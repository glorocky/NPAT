NPAT AI Rulebook v1.0
I propose we organize it into these sections.

1. Market Regime Engine

First question every morning:

Trend Day
Range Day
Gap Up
Gap Down
Expiry Day
Event Day
High Volatility
Low Volatility

No signal is generated until the market regime is identified.

2. Market Context Engine

This becomes the brain.

Inputs:

Long Build-up
Long Unwinding
Short Build-up
Short Covering
OI Change
Volume Change
LTP Change
IV Change
VIX
ATR

Output:

Bullish
Bearish
Neutral

3. Heavyweight Index Engine

Monitor the largest NIFTY contributors.

Initially:

HDFC Bank
Reliance
ICICI Bank
Bharti Airtel
Infosys
TCS
L&T
SBI
Axis Bank
AI should answer:
Are the heavyweights supporting the index move?

4. Option Intelligence Engine

For every option:

Calculate:

Delta
Gamma
Theta
Vega
IV

Then translate them into plain English.
Example:
This option may lose approximately ₹18–₹22 today due to time decay if the market remains sideways.

5. Volatility Engine

Using VIX:

Expected Day Range
Remaining Day Range
Volatility Expansion
Volatility Contraction
Example:
Expected move remaining: 42 points.
A 120-point target is unrealistic today.

. Liquidity Engine
Identify:

Call Wall
Put Wall
Max Pain
High OI Zones
OI Shift
Volume Shift

7. Risk Engine

Before every signal:

Check:

Risk/Reward ≥ 1:2
Theta acceptable?
IV acceptable?
Enough remaining expected move?
Sufficient liquidity?

If not:

NO TRADE

. Confidence Engine

Each engine contributes.

Example:

Engine	Weight
Market Regime	15%
Market Context	20%
Heavyweights	15%
Option Intelligence	15%
Liquidity	15%
Volatility	10%
Risk	10%
Final output:
Confidence = 0–100

9. Explainability Engine

Every signal must answer:

Why?
What supports it?
What could invalidate it?

For example:

UY PUT

Confidence: 88%

Reason:
✔ Long Unwinding
✔ Fresh Short Build-up
✔ Reliance below VWAP
✔ HDFC Bank weak
✔ VIX rising
✔ Expected move supports downside

Risk:
Medium

Invalidation:
NIFTY closes above 25,680

10. Capital Protection Engine

This is the final gate.

It asks:

"If this were my own money, would I take this trade?"

If the answer is No, NPAT blocks the signal.

Trade Memory Engine

Most systems treat every trade independently.

NPAT shouldn't.

It should remember the last few signals.

Example:

Three bullish signals failed in the last hour.
Market is ignoring bullish setups.
Current bullish signal should have lower confidence.

Likewise, if the market has respected bearish signals consistently, confidence in the next bearish signal can increase.

This isn't predicting the future—it's adapting to how the market is behaving today, which is often different from yesterday.
